Why I chose IT

Honestly, because I like making my own life easier. I automate and optimise the annoying parts of my work, and then I am trying to sell those solutions to other people who have the same problem.

I really believe that to sell a solution to a problem, you first have to run into that problem yourself. That's why I build tools for my own work first, and only then turn them into products.

My story

Ever since I was a kid, I've wanted to build my own little websites and tools, just by default. Before AI existed, that meant putting together things like Telegram bots in my spare time, mostly just to see if I could make them work. Around 2020 I got a bit into crypto, and seeing how much power people with solid programming skills had is what really pulled me toward the field.

From there I started automating small, annoying things around me and tried actually selling them - with mixed results. Once AI came along, that got a lot easier. The clearest example: social housing felt impossible to find, so I built myself a dashboard to track listings. It solved my own problem, but then came the real mind shift: what if this could help others too? I emailed a random company already working in that space, pitched my solution, and days later we were actually collaborating.

That one decision - from solving my own problem to selling the solution - was a massive shift in how I saw things. It's exactly why I chose ICT: not just to build for myself, but to turn ideas into real impact for others.

My Golden Circle

Why

I always have plenty of ideas, but sometimes it's hard to bring them to life on my own. That's exactly why I'm drawn to ICT: I want to build solutions that make people's lives easier, optimise their processes, and help turn ideas into something real and useful.

How

By learning modern technologies, building my own projects and practising on real-world cases, so I keep improving my programming skills and analytical thinking.

What

I study HBO-ICT at HZ University of Applied Sciences, and will soon be working as a developer who builds user-friendly digital products and automates processes for businesses and people.

Why HZ, and the long term

I picked HZ specifically because of its practice-first setup: a small enough program that I actually get feedback from teachers, and a location close enough to my home.

On top of that, I want to build a real network here: meet new people, find others who think the same way I do, and maybe even find a team to build something with along the way - not just get a diploma and leave.

Long term, I want to be a confident fullstack developer who defaults to tests and CI/CD instead of skipping them, with freelance work as steady income next to a regular job - not a side hustle I'm squeezing in, but a real second track. HZ is where I'm building the technical base and the network to get there.

What I enjoy the most

  • Automating boring, repetitive tasks.
  • Optimising processes so they take less time.
  • Trying to build side projects at home.
  • Turning a small tool into something other people pay for.
